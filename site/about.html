How I got here

From Python Turtle to a vehicle bus

I started programming in 2021 through TechSparx, a small training school in Bangalore that taught me over video call in one-on-one sessions. The first course was pure fundamentals — loops, conditionals, functions — and it ended with a thousand-line Python Turtle drawing that I was unreasonably proud of. The next two levels got progressively less decorative: a bulk email sender, then a standalone desktop application with a Tkinter front end over a MySQL database. By the end I understood that a program is not a script you run once but a thing other people have to use.

Code2College took that further. Their embedded systems course put me in front of a Raspberry Pi with a GPIO header and a pile of components, and I built a vehicle dashboard GUI in C++ and Qt Creator that actually responded to physical circuits I had wired myself. Their Elite program taught the parts of software nobody mentions in a tutorial — version control, Jira tickets, code review, testing. Their AI program, four months and a hundred hours long, is where I built my first genuinely full-stack product.

Three summers at Polaris turned that into professional work. Writing C++ and QML inside a codebase that hundreds of engineers depend on is a different discipline from writing it alone: your mistakes are expensive, your abstractions outlive you, and the most valuable skill is being able to read somebody else’s code accurately before you touch it.

I am now a first-year Computer Science student at The University of Texas at Austin, Class of 2030. I chose UT for its systems and architecture faculty and for the fact that it is twenty minutes from where I grew up, which means the communities I have been part of for years are still within reach.
